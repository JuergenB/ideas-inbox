## ~0.5% of monthly uniques pay; ~3–4% hit the wall; ~250–1,000 subscribers per 1M pageviews

- **Source:** Nieman Journalism Lab, "If my newspaper puts up a metered paywall, how many people will pay? Here's some data" (2014) — figures widely re-cited; surfaced here via WebPublisherPro.
- **URL (secondary, accessible):** https://webpublisherpro.com/how-does-your-conversion-rate-compare-to-other-publishers/
- **URL (primary):** https://www.niemanlab.org/2014/05/if-my-newspaper-puts-up-a-metered-paywall-how-many-people-will-pay-heres-some-data/ *(returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch on 2026-06-01; cited from search synthesis + WebPublisherPro corroboration)*
- **Claim as used:** "About 3–4% of a site's monthly unique visitors will hit a paywall, and about 0.5% of monthly uniques will actually pay. With 1 million pageviews monthly, a top performer might convert ~1,000 paid subscribers/month; the average is ~250."
- **Used in deck:** slides "Paywalls Need Volume AND Loyalty," "What The Benchmarks Say," "Volume: Two Orders of Magnitude Short."

## Anonymous→subscriber ~2.2% (top performers); 1–3% typical; registered→subscriber ~19%

- **URL:** https://theaudiencers.com/the-conversion-funnel-benchmark-report-q3-2023/
- **URL:** https://theaudiencers.com/the-conversion-funnel-benchmark-report-compare-your-performance-to-85-digital-publishing-brands/
- **Claim as used:** "Average conversion from registered user to subscriber ~19%; top performers exceed ~2.2% from anonymous visitor to subscriber; metered models typically convert 1–3% of total visitors."
- **Used in deck:** "How The Model Works," "What The Benchmarks Say."

## Only 20% of Americans pay for any online news; growth has plateaued

- **Source:** Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism, Digital News Report 2025.
- **URL:** https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary
- **URL (full PDF):** https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf
- **Claim as used:** "A fifth (20%) pay for online news in the United States. Across 20 wealthier nations the share paying is unchanged at ~18% — payment growth has plateaued. Norway tops the table at 42%."
- **Used in deck:** background on total addressable willingness-to-pay (the ceiling is low even for news, a category with far stronger paywall fit than arts discovery).

## Donation/membership fits small niche audiences better than a paywall

- **Source:** Elegant Themes, "What is a Paywall and When Should You Use One?"; Donorbox, "How To Secure Donations For Your Publication."
- **URL:** https://www.elegantthemes.com/blog/wordpress/what-is-a-paywall-and-when-should-you-use-one

- **Claim as used:** "Most small publications struggle to meet paywall criteria and a paywall ends up turning readers away rather than making money; donation / pay-what-you-want models suit dedicated niche audiences better and build trust."

## Hard paywalls can cut pageviews sharply after launch (supporting cost argument)

- **Source:** Our-Hometown, "The Impact of a Paywall on Website Traffic."

## Internal data — NotRealArt.com (UserMaven)

## ~5,000 visits/month; Period-1 cohort retention ~1.1%

- **Source:** UserMaven retention report for NotRealArt.com (screenshot provided by Juergen Berkessel).
- **Retrieved:** 2026-06-01.
- **Data:** Cohort retention table, Nov 2025 – Jun 2026. Average row: 49,538 users; Period 0 = 100%; **Period 1 = 1.1%**, Period 2 = 0.4%, Period 3 = 0.3%, Period 4 = 0.2%, Period 5 = 0.1%. Monthly cohort sizes ranged 4,382 (Dec 2025) to 14,096 (Feb 2026); recent stable months ~4,750–8,600 new visitors. Monthly visits ~5,000 (author-stated baseline).
- **Per-cohort Period-1 retention:** Nov 1.5%, Dec 2.0%, Jan 1.7%, Feb 0.8%, Mar 1.3%, Apr 0.9%, May 0.1%.
- **Interpretation:** ~1.1% average return rate ⇒ roughly 1 in 90 visitors returns even once. Benchmark loyalty for paywall-viable news sites is 30–40% returning. NRA is ~1/30th of that.
- **Caveat / next step:** UserMaven's repeat-visit reporting is limited. GA4 Retention / Cohort exploration (and returning-user %, engaged sessions, pages/session) would corroborate or refine the ~1.1% figure. See README "What Would Strengthen This Analysis."
- **Used in deck:** "Loyalty: Almost No One Comes Back," "Do The Math," "We're Not That Kind Of Site."

## GA4 corroboration — returning visitors 8.7% of activity; new-visitor retention 0.9% (day 1) → 0.1% (day 30)

- **Source:** Google Analytics 4, property "Not Real Art - GA4" (account Crewest Studio) — Retention overview report, exported to CSV.
- **Date range:** May 5 – Jun 1, 2026 (28 days); retention curve block spans Apr 21 – Jun 1, 2026 (42 days).
- **Retrieved:** 2026-06-02 (manual GA4 CSV export by Juergen Berkessel).
- **Raw data file:** [`research/data/ga4-retention-overview-2026-05.csv`](data/ga4-retention-overview-2026-05.csv); chart [`research/data/nra-new-vs-returning-2026-05.png`](data/nra-new-vs-returning-2026-05.png).
- **Data:** New users (28-day sum) = 3,339 (~119/day); Returning users (28-day sum) = 317 (~11/day) = **8.7%** of active-user-days. New-user retention curve (normalized): Day 1 = **0.88%**, Day 2 = 0.25%, Day 7 = 0.17%, Day 30 = 0.11%.
- **Method caveat:** the Retention-overview export gives *daily* new/returning counts; summing 28 days double-counts users who returned on multiple days, so 317 is an **upper bound** on unique monthly returning users (true unique count is somewhat lower, est. ~200–300). For an exact unique figure, use a GA4 "New/returning" exploration (dimension `newVsReturning` + metric `activeUsers`) or the GA4 Data API — see [`docs/reference/ga4-api.md`](../../../docs/reference/ga4-api.md).
- **Interpretation:** GA4 corroborates UserMaven from two independent angles. (1) New-visitor cohort retention (~0.9% day-1) matches UserMaven's ~1.1% Period-1 — new visitors almost never come back. (2) The broader "returning share" (8.7% of activity) is a small, roughly-flat *stock* of pre-existing repeat visitors (~11/day), not a growing flow of newly-loyal readers — and still far below the 30–40% returning share paywall-viable sites have.
- **Used in deck:** "Loyalty: Almost No One Comes Back" (GA4 corroboration line), "Sources & References."

## Comparison-table archetypes (deck slide "We're Not That Kind Of Site")

- **The New York Times** (~50–100M+ monthly visits; 11M+ subscribers; daily-habit national news) and **The Atlantic** (tens of millions of monthly visits; high repeat longform readership) are real, named examples of metered-paywall success. Figures are order-of-magnitude, drawn from public reporting on each publisher's scale and subscriber base.
- **"Regional daily"** (~0.5–2M monthly pageviews, local daily-habit readers) and **"Niche trade pub"** (smaller traffic, must-read professional content often expensed) are labeled **archetypes**, not specific named publishers — illustrative of the two structural patterns under which meters work at smaller scale (local monopoly on must-have news; job-critical professional content).
