## TL;DR

Arterial is running a fall fundraising campaign on social media. The obstacle is not that people dislike Arterial. It is that almost nobody knows it exists: they know Not Real Art, or they know Artsville USA, and they have no idea there is a non-profit holding them together.

So the plan puts the introduction first and the ask second. **One month of posts that only introduce Arterial, then two months of campaign posts that invite support warmly.** Alongside it, shared Instagram posts with partners outside the family, which put the campaign in front of audiences that have never heard of the organisation.

This is a discussion paper. It exists to be argued with, and specifically to get a fundraising practitioner's read on the judgement calls inside it.

## The problem the campaign has to solve first

Arterial.org is the registered 501(c)(3) behind Not Real Art, Artsville USA, Classic Black and the rest. That relationship is invisible to almost everyone who follows those brands.

It matters for fundraising in a very direct way. A person who has never met the organisation has no reason to give to it, and an ask that arrives before the introduction reads as a stranger asking for money. Whatever else the campaign does, it has to close that gap before it asks for anything.

## The approach: introduce, amplify, then invite

Three kinds of post, in that order of emphasis.

| Stage | What the posts do | What they ask for |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduce** | Who Arterial is, what sits under it, the people and programmes, the work that already exists | Nothing. Follow along, read, meet the work |
| **Amplify** | Why this work matters, told through a person or a project | Share it, tag someone, tell a friend |
| **Invite** | What support pays for, and what it makes possible | A gift, asked for directly and warmly |

Two things are deliberate here.

**Spreading the word is treated as real support**, not as the thing you offer people who cannot give. For an organisation whose problem is that nobody knows it exists, a share is worth a great deal.

**The ask is warm, never pressured.** Direct language is fine and welcome: "you can help fund this", "the campaign runs through December 15". Countdown clocks, "we are falling short", and last-chance framing are out. That is a judgement call, and it is the first thing we would like challenged.

## The shape: two campaigns over three months

- **Month 1 - Meet Arterial.** Posts that only introduce, published often, so the picture accumulates. No money language anywhere in the month.
- **Months 2 and 3 - The fall campaign.** Why the work matters, help us spread the word, and warm direct invitations to give.

Month one does not stop working when month two starts. Those posts have no expiry date in them, so they keep introducing Arterial to new people for as long as they are up.

## The growth idea: collaborator posts

On Instagram, two accounts can publish a single shared post. It lands on both feeds and reaches both audiences, with no advertising spend.

The easy half is Arterial's own family, since Not Real Art and Artsville USA already have followings. The more interesting half is **partners from outside the family** - artists, arts organisations, aligned non-profits. Each one reaches people who have never heard of Arterial, and it arrives as a recommendation from someone they already follow rather than as an advert.

Choosing those partners well is a relationships question, not a software question, and it is the part of this plan where outside judgement is worth the most.

## How the posts get made

The posting is done with **PolyWiz**, a tool Polymash has built. It takes one source - a web page or a PDF - and writes a few months of social posts from it, in the organisation's own voice, spread across Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, Pinterest and others. Nothing publishes automatically: every post is reviewed and edited by a person before it goes out.

Two new campaign styles are being built for exactly the plan above, an introduction campaign and a fundraising campaign. They are being built now, which is why this conversation is worth having now rather than after.

## What we would like to discuss

1. Is a full month of introduction before any ask the right balance, or too long a wait?
2. Is warm-but-never-pressured the right line, or does it leave money on the table?
3. Which partners outside the family should we approach for shared posts?
4. What normally works in a fall campaign that this plan does not mention at all?

## What we are asking for

A working session, separate from the first conversation, to think through where else this approach applies and to help shape the two campaign styles while they are still being built.
