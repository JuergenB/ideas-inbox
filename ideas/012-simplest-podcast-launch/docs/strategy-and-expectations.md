## The honest headline

The *mechanics* of launching a podcast are now genuinely easy (that's the whole deck). The hard
part was never the software — it's **audience**. A brand-new interview show, published once a
month, starting from zero subscribers, will grow **slowly** — realistically **very little in the
first year**. That's not a knock on Lynda or the concept; it's the base rate for the format. The
deck intentionally doesn't say this, because the deck's job is to remove the *fear of the how*.
This conversation is where the *strategy* gets set.

## Motivation check — the fork to surface gently

Before committing, it's worth understanding what success means to her. Two very different motives:

1. **"I love interviewing people / I want to have these conversations."**
   → **Great reason to host her own show.** The reward is intrinsic and per-episode. Slow audience
   growth barely matters, because the value is the conversation itself and a growing body of work
   she owns. Proceed with the Riverside setup; enjoyment is the metric.

2. **"I want to get my message / *Life Unlimited* out to as many people as possible."**
   → **Hosting a new show is one of the slowest ways to do that.** A better-fitting strategy is
   **podcast *guesting*** — appearing on *other people's already-established* shows, where the
   audience already exists. One good guest spot on an established wellness/psychology podcast can
   reach more of the right people in an hour than a year of her own monthly episodes.

These aren't mutually exclusive — many people do both. But knowing which one is her *primary*
driver changes the recommendation. If it's #2, lead with guesting and treat her own show as a
long-game companion, not the main vehicle.

## Realistic expectations to set (kindly, explicitly)

- **Cadence:** one episode/month is a slow drumbeat. It's sustainable and low-pressure (a plus for
  a busy practice), but discovery algorithms and habit-formation reward *frequency*. Monthly
  shows grow slower than weekly ones. Set the expectation, don't fight it.
- **Year one:** expect small numbers. Downloads in the tens-to-low-hundreds per episode is normal
  and *fine* for a niche, values-driven show. Frame this as success-by-depth, not reach.
- **Starting audience:** ~zero. Early listeners will come from *her existing* network — her
  clients, mailing list, social following, and her guests' audiences — not from cold discovery on
  Spotify. The website embed and cross-promotion (guests sharing their episode) matter more than
  the app charts.
- **Where growth actually comes from:** guests who share their own episode; her promoting each
  episode to her list/socials; and — if reach is the goal — her *own* guesting appearances
  pointing back to the show. Not from publishing and waiting.

## How I'd frame the conversation with Lynda

- Open with the deck's promise: the technical fear is gone, one tool, a few clicks. Let her feel
  that starting is genuinely low-friction.
- Then ask the motivation question directly but warmly: *"When you picture this a year from now,
  what makes it feel worth it — the conversations themselves, or getting your work in front of
  more people?"* Her answer routes the strategy.
- If it's the conversations: green-light the show, keep it light, protect the joy, don't tie it to
  vanity metrics.
- If it's reach: introduce guesting as the faster lever, and position her own show as the
  owned-home-base that guesting drives people back to.
- Set the year-one expectation explicitly so she's not disappointed at month four. "Slow and
  sustained, by design" is the honest and healthy framing — and it's very much on-brand with
  *The Best is Yet to Come*.

## Practical recommendation (my current lean)

- Proceed with the **Riverside + free Spotify for Creators** setup regardless — it's cheap
  (~$29/mo), removes every technical excuse, and is the right home base either way.
- Web person does the one-time setup (`setup-runbook.md`) and the site embed.
- **Pair the show with a guesting effort** if reach matters to her — that's where the audience is.
- Revisit cadence after ~3–4 episodes; if she's enjoying it and wants more reach, moving toward
  every-other-week is the single biggest growth lever available.
