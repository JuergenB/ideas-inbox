## The Situation

Rokas at Guideless noticed Juergen had signed up but barely used the product, and reached out. Juergen praised the tool's elegant, zero-editing workflow but flagged the one paid tier — **$29/mo** — as out of reach for an add-on he reaches for alongside Tella and Screen Studio. Rokas came back with the direct question: *"What price point would you be comfortable with?"*

Rather than just hand over a personal number (which reads as fishing for a discount), this answers from **product positioning**: where Guideless actually sits in the category, and a tier structure that would open a segment Guideless can't reach today.

## The Recommendation — Three Tiers, Not Two

Guideless intentionally trades editing flexibility for speed. That makes it a **complementary, quick-hit utility**, not a replacement for a primary editor like Tella — yet it's priced at or above tools that give far more control. Today the ladder jumps straight from Free (3 guides) to a single $29 Team plan, with nothing for the solo buyer in between.

The suggestion is to **add one lane**, not restructure everything:

| Tier | Price | Move | Role |
|------|-------|------|------|
| **Free** | $0 (3 guides) | Keep as-is | Onboarding — "it just works" moment |
| **Solo / Creator** | **~$9/user/mo** annual | **New** | The core magic, no watermark, MP4 export, basic analytics — limited by *volume* (a monthly guide cap, single workspace), not by features |
| **Team / Pro** | ~$29/user/mo annual | Reframe existing plan | Now clearly the *team* tier: unlimited guides, workspaces, roles, advanced analytics, brand kits, priority support |

The $9 Solo tier sits just above Tella's rock-bottom annual band and well below Loom Business and the current $29 plan — the natural home for "I love it, but it's my second tool." It captures solo creators, consultants, and founders who already pay for Loom/Tella/Screen Studio, and acts as a **top of funnel into the Team plan** rather than a leak from it. No discount to the existing $29 tier.

*(An earlier draft of this thinking carried a fourth Business/Enterprise tier; that was dropped — the load-bearing move is simply injecting the Solo lane.)*

## Why It's Captured Here

This started as a real reply Juergen needed to send, but the positioning work — competitive landscape, where a "pay for speed vs. pay for control" product should price, how to add a segment without diluting an existing tier — is reusable product-strategy thinking worth keeping. The deck is Polymash-branded so it doubles as a small artifact of how Polymash thinks about positioning, should it ever be useful in a client context.
