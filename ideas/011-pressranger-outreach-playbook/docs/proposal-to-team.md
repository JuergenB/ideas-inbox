# Note to Scott & Elise — PressRanger as our outreach engine

*Draft in Juergen's voice. Send as email or drop in the Thursday agenda. Tighten as needed.*

**Subject: Let's actually use the PressRanger we already bought**

Scott, Elise —

So it turns out we've both been sitting on PressRanger lifetime accounts since some AppSumo afternoon we've each forgotten about. Classic. The good news is that "low cost" Scott mentioned is actually a one-time cost we already paid — the thing that usually runs $3,000 to $15,000 a year (Cision, Muck Rack, Prowly) we own outright.

Here's the part worth getting right: PressRanger is really two products in one box. There's a **media database + outreach CRM + AI pitch drafting** — that's the expensive lane everywhere else, and it's the one we need. And there's an **optional pay-per-release wire** (~$299) — cheaper than PR Newswire's real $1,500–$3,000 invoices, but not free, and frankly not the point. The point is the database and the ability to find and reach the right people *repeatedly*.

I'm not going to oversell it. The reviews are honest about the data being noisy — beats matched by keyword, a lot of "this person mentioned travel once" showing up in travel lists, and exports that hand you a name but not always a working email. So the move isn't "trust the list and hit send." It's: mine PressRanger for discovery, build our own curated contact list in Airtable (the central repository we keep saying we need), and let the system we already built handle the cadence.

Which gets to the actual gap. We've got a great production machine and we promote almost nothing through it. We schedule open calls and exhibitions and then lean on our own email list, which — as we said on Thursday — is too small to carry it. The grants worked because we ran Facebook ads to the landing pages. Everything else gets the organic-and-hope treatment.

So I've written up a **promotion playbook**: a press release when a call opens, when the show is announced, and when it goes live; listings on the sites where artists actually look; an email blast; paid social to an aligned landing page; targeted pitches to real arts journalists. Same steps every time, triggered automatically so it doesn't depend on someone remembering.

**The ask:** Elise, you're the closest to artist outreach and the eventual fundraising side, so I'd love you to be the one to kick the tires — not "is this tool good in the abstract," but "can it actually find and help us reach people for a real open call." *Art of Resistance* is the obvious first test. Scott's already sharing the password; I've got codes too, and pooling them raises our limits.

Phase two is the fall fundraising push — same engine, pointed at donors and impact stories once the books and DAF groundwork are in place. But that's later. First let's just promote a call properly for once.

Full write-up, the tool comparison, and the playbook are in the idea folder. Twenty minutes on Thursday to agree the playbook and the Airtable schema and we're off.

— Juergen
